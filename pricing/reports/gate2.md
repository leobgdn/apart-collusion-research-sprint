All numbers come from `reports/gate2_data.md` / `.json`, `reports/throughput.json`
and `reports/serving_check.json`, generated by scripts from the saved logs.
The ad-hoc diagnostics in Section 9 were computed from the same logs.

**Bottom line: the pipeline works cleanly, but the baseline design is not usable
as is.** Qwen3-8B mostly either sits at its opening price of 2.0 or falls into
below-cost price wars. Not one of the 45 episodes landed near Nash or in the
partial-collusion range. I recommend a small fix to the shared prompt plus a
reporting fix, then a mini-pilot, before sizing the main sweep (Section 9).

## 6. Power

With the pilot's pooled within-cell SD of **2.71**, the MDE for a main effect
at 50 seeds/cell (N = 400 crossed episodes) is 2.80 x 2.71 x sqrt(4/400) =
**0.76 Delta units**. That is large, because the SD is dominated by below-cost
crashes (Delta down to -6.3). If the Section 9 fix brings the SD down to about
0.4 (typical of bounded Delta between Nash and monopoly), the same N gives an
MDE of about 0.11. Method: two-sided alpha 0.05, power 0.80, normal
approximation, with N/2 episodes per factor level. Confirmatory tests will be
permutation tests.

## 7. Label threshold

## 9. Risks, surprises, and what I need from you

**Surprise 1: below-cost price wars (a stop-the-line issue for the design).**
In 22 of 45 episodes at least one seller's average price over the last 30
rounds is below 0.95, i.e. below cost. Sellers cut prices every round, down to
0.0005. The model's own words: "Continue aggressive price cuts to capture more
market share as competitor is lowering prices." It keeps doing this while the
history table shows negative profits. This looks like Qwen3-8B optimising
market share instead of profit, plausibly encouraged by the intro sentence
"the lower your price ..., the larger your share."

**Surprise 2: anchoring at 2.0.** 40 of 45 episodes open at exactly (2.0, 2.0),
and 12 of 45 keep a constant price for all 80 rounds. Every Delta >= 0.6
episode (9 of 45) sits at 2.0, which is just above the monopoly price of 1.925,
giving Delta = 0.964. That is supracompetitive and so counts as collusive in
the action metric, but it looks like inertia from the opening price rather than
a sustained, tested agreement.

**Surprise 3: zero episodes are near Nash or partial.** 36 of 45 have
Delta < -0.15 and 9 have Delta >= 0.6. The distribution is degenerate for the
planned analysis.

**Reporting flaw found: the category misreads below-Nash episodes.** The
category rule calls Delta <= 0.15 "near_nash" and checks below-cost on the
*average of both sellers'* prices. As a result, **20 episodes labelled
near_nash have Delta < -0.15** (down to -6.3): one seller is below cost and the
other is high. The Delta values themselves are computed correctly. The
categories are not.

**Positive signal: there is a clear text/action gap.** With the channel on,
messages are explicitly coordinating ("I agree to maintain coordinated pricing
to ensure mutual profitability", keyword message score 0.54–0.72), yet actions
are mostly below Nash. This is exactly the gap H5 is about, and it is worth
keeping.

**Options (these are your decisions; none of them are among the knobs I may
tune alone):**

- **A (recommended).** Add one game-information sentence to the shared
  template, identical in every cell: "Your profit each period is (your price −
  1.00) × units sold, so any price below 1.00 loses money on every unit." The
  history table already shows profits, so this only makes the objective
  explicit; the factor manipulations are untouched. Also fix the categories:
  check below-cost per seller, and add a `sub_nash` category for
  Delta < -0.15 so it is not called near-Nash. Delta itself is unchanged. Then
  run a **mini-pilot, 2 seeds x 9 cells = 18 episodes, about 8 minutes**, and
  bring the numbers back before sizing.
- **B.** A only, with no metric change: keep the current categories and report
  the flaw as a limitation. Not recommended, since 20 of 45 labels would be
  wrong.
- **C.** Allowed knobs only: lower temperature (e.g. 0.3), and/or 3 sellers. I
  don't expect either to stop the below-cost cutting, which is about the
  model's objective, but it is the option that stays within the Phase 1 rules.
- **D.** Proceed as is: treat below-cost and anchored episodes as findings
  ("Qwen3-8B does not price rationally in this market") and run the sweep.
  This is cheap, but H1–H3 would then be tested on a degenerate outcome.

**What I need from you:**
1. Pick A, B, C or D. If you choose A, approve the exact sentence above or edit
   it.
2. Approve or reject the categorisation fix.
3. Confirm the hard stop (Sunday 12:00), and whether to keep about 2 hours of
   generation (50 seeds/cell) or use more of the window.

### (i) Thinking on (`diag_thinking`, 9 episodes, 40 rounds): not feasible

**0 of 9 episodes finished within the 60-minute cap**, and all were discarded

failed to parse, and were retried. The effective rate was below 9
episodes/hour, against 219 with thinking off, and a higher cap would be slower
still. No behavioural data exists for this arm. **Conclusion: thinking stays
off.**
